### In the paper
## Ready to start preservation work.

On September 10, 2026, the Hendersonville Standard and Main Street Media published a story by Abbey Nutter on the transfer of the Latimer House and what the Friends of the Latimer House plan to do first. It is worth reading in full: [Friends of the Latimer House ready to start preservation work](https://mainstreetmediatn.com/articles/headlines-newsletter/friends-of-the-latimer-house-ready-to-start-preservation-work/).

The story follows the house from the 1790's, when Charles Latimer raised it from logs cut on this ground, on land his father Colonel Jonathan Latimer had given him, to the deed recorded on August 27. Jim Latimer, a direct descendant of the Colonel and President of the organization, spoke for the Friends.

### The years it waited
## Lucky it is still standing.

In 2016 William Brown left five hundred thousand dollars to establish a public park here, with this house as its centerpiece. For nearly ten years after that, the house stood in neglect.

> "The doors have been kicked in. The windows have been broken out. People have been in the house."
>
> Jim Latimer, to Main Street Media

The money Mr. Brown left for the house was wrongfully confiscated and combined into the school system's education technology trust, a fund it was never left to. Citizens and a small group of county commissioners pressed for years to see his wishes honored. The money was separated back out and returned to the county in 2025. The Commission approved the conveyance of the house and the transfer of the fund on August 17, 2026, the Board of Education approved it on August 18, and the deed was recorded on August 27.

### What comes next
## A way in, and a policy.

The first work is an entrance to the property and to the house. The organization has opened bank accounts for the property, and the process of insuring the house is underway. Volunteer days will be announced once that coverage is in place.

> "He made one simple request. He just wanted the house preserved as the center of a park bearing his wife's and his name."
>
> Jim Latimer, on Mr. Brown

Thank you to Abbey Nutter and Main Street Media for covering it.
